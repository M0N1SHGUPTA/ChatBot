# 01  Executive Company Overview
A single reference for JIBB’s identity, service capabilities, bilateral programmes, sector focus and chatbot knowledge.
## What is JIBB?
Japan India Business Bureau (JIBB) is an independent non-profit bilateral organisation that works to strengthen economic, industrial and cultural ties between Japan and India. It connects companies, governments, institutions, industry bodies, academic organisations, researchers, startups and experts so that cross-border opportunities can progress from initial discussions to structured business relationships and practical implementation.

JIBB combines market intelligence, partner identification, business matching, market-entry planning, investment and joint-venture facilitation, local coordination, sales and ecosystem development, and continuing corporate support. Its role is not limited to introducing two organisations. It aims to help the parties understand the opportunity, assess possible partners, navigate the business environment and establish a workable path for collaboration.

The organisation operates across a Tokyo–Noida axis, linking Japanese technology and industrial capabilities with Indian market opportunities, manufacturing ecosystems, government stakeholders and local business networks. Its bilateral role also supports Indian organisations seeking Japanese partners, technology, market access and institutional relationships.
## JIBB at a glance
Organisation: Japan India Business Bureau (JIBB) / 日印ビジネスビューロー.

Organisation type: Independent non-profit bilateral organisation.

Geographic focus: Japan–India business, manufacturing, technology, investment and innovation collaboration.

Headline indicators used in JIBB’s services profile: 500+ business connections; US$100M+ facilitated deals; 8 focus sectors; 20+ years of Indo-Japanese expertise.

Main operating locations: Tokyo, Japan and Noida, India.
## What JIBB is positioned to help achieve
JIBB’s work is designed to create a practical bridge between market opportunity and execution. Depending on the engagement, this may involve understanding a sector, identifying prospective customers or partners, organising B2B meetings, evaluating a local counterpart, considering an entry structure, coordinating local establishment, or supporting the company after it has entered the market.

**Core positioning**  JIBB is a bilateral business facilitation and execution platform: it connects the right organisations, helps structure collaboration and supports companies as they establish and grow their India–Japan relationships.

# 05  Core Services: Research, Opportunity & Partnership
JIBB’s service offering is organised into eight integrated service lines. These services can be used individually or combined into a broader engagement, depending on the company’s market-entry stage, business objectives and local requirements.
## 1. Market Landscaping
Market Landscaping builds a structured view of an industry before a company commits significant time or capital. It examines the market as an ecosystem rather than simply collecting company names or headline market-size figures. The work can cover value-chain structure, demand drivers, customer segments, competitor positioning, new technologies, industry trends, regulations, supply chains and relevant government initiatives.

The purpose is to help the company understand where the market is developing, which parts of the value chain are relevant to its capabilities, where competition is concentrated and where structural gaps or unmet needs may create an opportunity. Market Landscaping gives decision-makers a more complete basis for deciding whether and how to pursue a market.
## 2. Opportunity Landscape / Assessment
Opportunity Assessment translates broad market information into specific, prioritised opportunities. It examines market size and growth, addressable demand, customer groups, segment attractiveness, competitive intensity and possible routes to revenue. JIBB’s materials refer to TAM, SAM and SOM frameworks, together with top-down and bottom-up analysis, to make the opportunity more concrete.

The output is intended to help companies decide which segments deserve attention first, which customer groups to approach, what capabilities may be required and how realistic commercial pathways might be structured. The assessment can also help compare alternatives and distinguish attractive market potential from an opportunity that is difficult to access or execute.
## 3. Partner Identification & Ecosystem Building
Partner Identification focuses on finding relevant organisations for a defined business requirement. Depending on the company’s objectives, this may include customers, suppliers, distributors, contract manufacturers, technology providers, investors, research partners or joint-venture candidates. JIBB’s role is to map the ecosystem and narrow the field to organisations whose capabilities and interests may fit the requirement.

The assessment may consider technical and operational capability, business credibility, market access, customer relationships, scale, strategic fit, cultural alignment and willingness to collaborate. The aim is to create a basis for meaningful discussions rather than relying only on generic networking or directory listings.
## 4. Due Diligence & Deal Structuring
Due Diligence & Deal Structuring helps organisations examine the commercial and organisational factors that can affect a potential partnership or investment. The scope may include financial position, operations, governance, market positioning, technical capabilities, ownership and relevant business risks. The work helps decision-makers identify issues that should be understood before entering negotiations or committing resources.

JIBB can also support commercial negotiations and the development of a suitable transaction or collaboration structure. Potential structures may include joint ventures, strategic alliances, technology licensing and other partnership arrangements. Legal, tax and regulatory conclusions must be confirmed by appropriately qualified advisers for the specific transaction.
# 06  Core Services: Market Entry, Execution & Corporate Support
## 5. Go-to-Market Strategy & Entry Blueprint
Go-to-Market Strategy converts a company’s interest in a market into a structured entry plan. It considers the company’s objectives, preferred level of control, investment capacity, target customers, competitive position, value proposition, pricing logic, sales channels and local operating requirements. It also helps determine whether the company should enter independently, work with a local partner, use a distributor, license technology or consider another entry model.

The purpose is to give management an execution-ready roadmap: what the company intends to offer, to whom, through which channel, with what local structure and in what sequence. A strong entry blueprint links the market opportunity to the practical decisions that must be made before launch.
## 6. Local Presence & Market Entry Execution
Local Presence & Market Entry Execution supports companies as they move from planning into establishment and day-to-day activity. The scope may include location and state evaluation, local representation, office or operating-entity setup coordination, partner and vendor identification, government liaison, approvals and licences, incentive advisory, recruitment coordination and ongoing local communication.

For companies establishing manufacturing, R&D, sales or service operations, the work may extend to site selection, local sourcing, infrastructure and utility coordination, project management and issue resolution. JIBB’s role is to help the company coordinate the local ecosystem and navigate practical steps; specific legal, engineering, tax or regulatory decisions remain subject to the relevant professional and statutory processes.
## 7. Sales, Marketing & Ecosystem Enablement
Sales, Marketing & Ecosystem Enablement helps companies create visibility and build commercial momentum after they have identified a market or established a local presence. Activities may include distributor onboarding, sales-channel development, customer outreach, marketing support, exhibition participation, curated B2B meetings, business events and engagement with relevant industry bodies.

The objective is to connect the company’s offering with the right market participants and create opportunities for commercial conversations. This service can be particularly relevant for businesses that have a product or technology ready for the Indian market but need introductions, local market understanding and structured business-development support.
## 8. Back Office & Corporate Support
Back Office & Corporate Support covers continuing operational services that help companies manage their presence in India or Japan. The listed areas include entity setup coordination, accounting, taxation, corporate secretarial services, HR and payroll, compliance management, M&A support and director-level assistance across the two countries.

These services support operational continuity after market entry. The exact scope depends on the company’s entity structure, activities, size and regulatory obligations. The chatbot should describe the service categories at a high level and route specific accounting, tax, legal or employment questions to the relevant JIBB contact or qualified professional.
# 07  JIBB Engagement & Delivery Approach
## A connected path from first contact to growth
JIBB’s delivery model is designed to guide an organisation from an initial introduction through facilitation and into longer-term growth. Not every engagement requires every step. A company seeking a distributor may need market and partner research followed by B2B meetings, while a company considering a manufacturing investment may require a broader sequence covering location analysis, incentives, approvals, setup, suppliers and project execution.
### Connect
The first stage is to understand the organisation, its business, its objectives and the kind of counterpart or opportunity it is seeking. JIBB can then identify relevant stakeholders across its Japan and India networks. The connection may involve a company, government body, industry association, academic institution, research organisation, startup or technical expert.
### Facilitate
Once relevant parties have been identified, JIBB can help structure the interaction. This may involve sharing market context, organising B2B meetings, supporting communication, coordinating delegations, preparing for negotiations, helping parties understand business expectations and supporting the exploration of possible commercial or institutional arrangements.
### Enable Growth
The growth stage focuses on what happens after the first connection or market-entry decision. Depending on the mandate, JIBB may support company registration coordination, local representation, market activation, partner onboarding, ongoing advisory, business development, operational coordination and policy or stakeholder engagement.
## Typical engagement sequence
Planning: clarify objectives, addressable market, growth potential, competition, organisation structure and intended distribution approach.

Validation: conduct market or location visits, verify assumptions, meet relevant stakeholders and assess whether the proposed opportunity fits the company’s capabilities.

Entry strategy: define the entry model, target customer and partner approach, location options, sales projections and operating requirements.

Project execution: coordinate approvals, incorporation, site selection, land or facility processes, incentives, recruitment, project management, vendors and support services as applicable.

Advisory and growth: support compliance awareness, policy updates, sales ramp-up, distributor selection, HR requirements, operational improvements and longer-term expansion.

**Engagement principle**  The scope, sequence, deliverables and timing should be defined for each engagement. The chatbot must not promise a standard delivery period or guaranteed business result.

# 12  Research & Innovation Hub: Purpose & Ecosystem
## What is the R&I Hub?
The JIBB Research & Innovation Hub (R&I Hub) is a Japan–India collaboration framework that connects industrial requirements with research expertise, technologies, startups, academic institutions and specialised technical capabilities. Its purpose is to bring the relevant people and resources together so that research and technology development can progress towards validation, industrial application and commercialisation.

The Hub is designed around the relationship between technology and market needs. Companies may have a specific industrial challenge or a need for a technology partner. Startups and researchers may have an idea, prototype or technical capability. Universities and Centres of Excellence may provide specialist knowledge, equipment or facilities. The Hub’s role is to help identify the relevant connection and structure a potential collaboration.
## Who the Hub connects
### Corporates
Corporates bring industrial challenges, manufacturing requirements, customer needs, technology roadmaps and market knowledge. They may seek technologies, research partners, prototypes, specialist testing capabilities or solutions that can be integrated into products and processes.
### Startups
Startups bring ideas, technologies, prototypes and entrepreneurial energy. They may seek technical expertise, product validation, industry mentors, specialised facilities, potential customers, strategic partners or routes to market.
### Academia, universities and researchers
Academic and research organisations contribute scientific knowledge, researchers, technical expertise, testing capabilities and talent. Through collaboration with industry, they can help define research topics, undertake experiments, develop prototypes and support validation.
### Centres of Excellence
Centres of Excellence (CoEs) and specialised institutions contribute domain-specific capabilities, laboratories, equipment, facilities and technical expertise. They can be relevant where a project requires resources that are not available within a participating company or research team.
# 13  R&I Hub: Workstreams, Development Pathway & Network
## Four R&I Hub workstreams
### 1. Technology Development
A participant brings an idea or technology and the Hub helps identify complementary teams. The parties can explore joint development, technical refinement, prototype development and validation. The emphasis is on matching the technology with appropriate knowledge and industrial requirements.
### 2. Joint Research
Japanese and Indian research teams can be connected around a common research topic. The model brings together research expertise, academic guidance, shared capabilities and a defined technical objective. Collaboration may produce research outputs, technical know-how, prototypes or a foundation for further industrial work.
### 3. Corporate Open Innovation
A corporate participant identifies a challenge or requirement. The Hub can help identify startups, technologies or research capabilities relevant to that challenge, support discussion and development, and explore collaboration pathways such as co-development, investment, acquisition or a joint venture where appropriate.
### 4. Technology & Market Readiness
Technology development is considered alongside market development. The pathway can include testing, validation, interaction with industry and early adopters, and refinement of the solution so that it is better aligned with real-world application and potential market needs.
## Research-to-commercialisation pathway
Research and feasibility: identify the technical question, relevant researchers, experiments, proof of concept and feasibility considerations.

Technology development: refine the concept, identify complementary expertise and develop prototypes or functional components.

Experimentation and validation: test performance, evaluate technical constraints and improve the solution based on findings.

Product development and market fit: refine the product against industrial requirements, user needs and commercial conditions.

Commercialisation: explore routes such as corporate collaboration, co-development, investment, acquisition, licensing or joint venture.
## Technology Readiness Levels
The Hub’s materials refer to a Technology Readiness Level (TRL) pathway from TRL 1 to TRL 9. The stages broadly move from early research and proof of concept, through prototype development and validation, to system integration, pilot operations and commercial application. The TRL framework helps participants discuss how mature a technology is and what type of support or validation may be needed next.
## Distributed capability network — examples
The distributed network concept can connect a company or startup with specialised capabilities across the wider academic and research ecosystem. Examples used in the model include IITs, NITs, IIITs, Delhi Technological University (DTU), Netaji Subhas University of Technology (NSUT), specialised research institutions and industry laboratories. These are examples of the types of institutions that may form part of a distributed capability network.

Organisations may participate by contributing domain expertise, specialised facilities, equipment, technical guidance or funding; sharing industrial challenges and technology roadmaps; or helping identify suitable academic, startup and corporate collaborators.

# 19  Market Entry Models, Offices & Key Terms
## Market-entry models supported by JIBB
JIBB helps companies understand and evaluate different ways of entering or operating in India. The appropriate model depends on business objectives, investment capacity, desired control, speed, local partner availability, regulatory requirements and long-term plans. JIBB can support the assessment and facilitation of the following models:

Representative Office (RO) — a representative presence for liaison, market exploration and permitted non-commercial activities.

Branch Office (BO) — an India presence for activities permitted under the relevant regulatory approvals.

Project Office (PO) — a presence established for the execution of a specific project.

Wholly Owned Subsidiary (WOS) — a locally incorporated entity owned by the foreign parent, subject to applicable rules.

Joint Venture (JV) — a business arrangement with an Indian partner that shares ownership, capabilities, responsibilities or investment.

Distributor / Channel Partner — a local partner that supports sales, market access and customer coverage.

Licensing / Technology Tie-up — an arrangement for technology, intellectual property or know-how to be used under agreed terms.

Contract Manufacturing — production through a local manufacturing partner rather than establishing all production assets directly.

Mergers & Acquisitions (M&A) — acquisition, merger or other transaction involving an existing business.

Greenfield Investment — establishing a new facility or operation from the ground up.

Brownfield Investment — investment in, expansion of or use of an existing facility or operating asset.

The chatbot may explain these models at a high level but must not recommend a structure as legally or commercially optimal without understanding the company’s circumstances and referring the matter for professional review.

## Key terms used in JIBB materials
TAM / SAM / SOM: Total Addressable Market, Serviceable Available Market and Serviceable Obtainable Market; frameworks for understanding market size and the portion a company may realistically serve.

OSAT: Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test; services that package and test semiconductor devices after wafer fabrication.

ATMP: Assembly, Testing, Marking and Packaging; a broad term for semiconductor back-end manufacturing processes.

CMP: Chemical Mechanical Planarization; a semiconductor manufacturing process used to smooth and planarise wafer surfaces.

CoE: Centre of Excellence; a specialised organisation or facility with particular technical expertise, equipment or research capabilities.

TRL: Technology Readiness Level; a scale used to describe the maturity of a technology from early research to system demonstration and commercial application.

ESDM: Electronics System Design and Manufacturing; the ecosystem covering electronics design, components and manufacturing.

PLI: Production Linked Incentive; a policy incentive framework linked to defined production or sales criteria in specified sectors.
# 20  JIBB Chatbot: Knowledge Architecture & Core Response Rules
## Purpose of the chatbot
The JIBB chatbot should function as a reliable first point of information about the organisation. It should explain what JIBB is, describe its services and sectors, guide users towards relevant programmes, provide approved information about membership and events, and help visitors understand how to make an enquiry. It should reduce the effort required to understand JIBB while directing specific business requirements to the appropriate human contact.

The chatbot is not a replacement for JIBB’s consultants, legal or tax advisers, government authorities, member relationship managers or event teams. Its job is to explain, orient and route. It should not make commitments on behalf of JIBB, guarantee outcomes, disclose confidential information or independently approve commercial arrangements.
## Primary user groups and likely intents
Japanese companies exploring India: market overview, sectors, entry models, partner search, local representation, exhibitions and operational support.

Indian companies exploring Japan: partner search, technology cooperation, market access, delegation opportunities and business matching.

Members and prospective members: membership tiers, benefits, member-to-member connections, events, advisory access and showcase participation.

Government and public institutions: delegation support, industry engagement, policy dialogue and investment facilitation.

Researchers, universities and startups: R&I Hub framework, technology development, joint research, Centres of Excellence and corporate open innovation.

Event participants: event purpose, country, JIBB’s role, delegation information and how to request registration details.
## Knowledge structure
The chatbot’s knowledge should be organised into distinct subject areas rather than one undifferentiated block of text. This makes it easier to retrieve the right detail and reduces the chance that information about one programme is mistakenly applied to another.

Organisation profile: identity, positioning, vision, mission, values, locations and contact information.

Services: the eight service lines, scope, typical activities and how a company may engage JIBB.

Sectors: the eight focus sectors and representative sub-sectors.

Programmes: JISC, R&I Hub and 365-Day Japan Showcase, each with its own role and boundaries.

Membership: tier names, benefits, event access, business matching and member privileges; fees are not included in this profile.

People and ecosystem: leadership, JISC committee members, companies, institutions and stakeholder organisations.

Events and knowledge: approved completed and upcoming events, delegations, articles and thought-leadership themes.

Market-entry guidance: the list of entry models and high-level explanations.
## Recommended answer construction
Identify the user’s intent. Determine whether the question concerns JIBB generally, a service, a sector, a programme, membership, a person, a stakeholder, an event or market-entry guidance.

Answer the direct question first. Begin with a plain-language response before adding supporting details.

Add only relevant context. Use the relevant service, sector or programme details; do not repeat the full company overview in every answer.

Clarify the boundary. Explain whether the information is a general description, a current event detail, an illustrative example or a matter requiring confirmation.

Provide a next step. Where appropriate, guide the user to submit an enquiry, describe their business requirement or request a meeting with JIBB.

Collect only information needed to understand the enquiry and route it to the right JIBB contact.

Do not ask users to submit sensitive personal, financial or identity information through a general chatbot conversation.

Escalate the enquiry to an authorised JIBB contact when the user requests a specific partner introduction, a confidential member list, a commercial quotation, membership pricing, a formal proposal, a legal or tax interpretation, an investment recommendation, a transaction or JV assessment, a regulatory approval view, an event registration or a change to a delegation schedule.
